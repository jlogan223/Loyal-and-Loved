Types of Cat Litter Explained: What Are Clumping, Silica, Wood and Paper Litters?

Cat litter falls into four main categories, each with different materials, absorbency methods, and environmental footprints. Understanding the difference before you buy will save you money and keep your cat using the tray consistently.

Clumping Cat Litter

Clumping litter, most commonly made from sodium bentonite clay, forms solid clumps when it contacts liquid. According to the American Association of Feline Practitioners (AAFP) 2023 guidelines, unscented clumping litter is the format most cats prefer when given a choice, primarily because it maintains a clean, dry surface around the clump. UK prices typically range from around £5 to £20 for a 10 kg bag. The main downside is weight, as bags of bentonite clay are heavy to carry, and the product is not biodegradable.

Silica Gel Cat Litter

Silica gel litter (sometimes called crystal litter) is made from silicon dioxide, the same desiccant material you find in those little sachets inside shoe boxes. It absorbs urine rapidly and traps odour molecules rather than just masking them. A single bag of around 3.8 litres typically lasts one cat two to four weeks, which can make the higher upfront cost (around £8 to £15) more reasonable over time. Silica litter does not clump; instead, solid waste sits on top for removal, and urine is absorbed invisibly. Some cats dislike the texture, particularly those with sensitive paws.

Wood Pellet Litter

Wood pellet litter is made from compressed sawdust, often a by-product of the timber industry. When wet, the pellets break down into sawdust, which falls to the bottom of a two-layer sifting tray. It is highly absorbent, naturally odour-controlling thanks to the wood's pine or cedarwood properties, and genuinely biodegradable. Prices start from around £6 for 15 litres. The main barrier is convincing your cat to accept the unfamiliar texture, particularly if they are used to fine-grain clay.

Paper-Based Litter

Paper litter is made from recycled paper pellets or granules. It is the softest underfoot, making it a common recommendation from vets for cats recovering from surgery or declawing (a practice now banned in the UK under the Animal Welfare Act 2006). It is highly absorbent but has comparatively weaker odour control than clumping or silica options. Expect to pay around £5 to £12 for a 10 litre bag.

How to Control Litter Box Odour: What Actually Works?

Effective litter box odour control comes down to litter choice, scooping frequency, tray placement, and ventilation rather than any single product. According to the International Cat Care charity (iCatCare), the primary cause of persistent odour is inadequate scooping frequency rather than the litter type itself.

  • Scoop solid waste daily. Even the best litter cannot overcome a tray that is not maintained. Once solid waste breaks down, ammonia compounds become much harder to eliminate.
  • Choose unscented litter. Strongly perfumed litters can deter cats from using the tray, which causes accidents elsewhere. Loyal & Loved found that most cats in our test panel showed a preference for unscented options, consistent with the AAFP 2023 guidelines.
  • Use a litter deodoriser powder sparingly. Products such as Arm and Hammer Cat Litter Deodoriser (approximately £5 to £8 on Amazon) can help between full tray changes, but they should not replace regular cleaning.
  • Place the tray in a ventilated spot. Avoid sealed cupboards or enclosed spaces where ammonia concentrations build up. A corner with some airflow makes a significant difference.
  • Consider an enclosed tray with a filter. Covered trays with activated carbon filters trap odour effectively. The Modkat Flip Litter Box (around £40 to £55) and several enclosed options on Amazon include replaceable carbon filters. Browse covered litter trays on Amazon.
  • Replace the tray itself periodically. Plastic litter trays absorb odours over time even after washing. According to Loyal & Loved's testing, replacing a tray every 12 to 18 months noticeably reduces baseline smell in the litter area.

How Deep Should You Fill the Litter Tray?

For clumping litters, a depth of 7 to 10 cm (approximately 3 to 4 inches) is the recommended fill level, giving clumps room to form fully without reaching the tray bottom. For non-clumping litters, 5 cm is typically sufficient.

According to iCatCare, most owners underfill their litter trays, which leads to clumps sticking to the base, urine pooling rather than absorbing, and more frequent full changes. A properly filled tray actually saves litter over time because it clumps more efficiently and reduces waste.

For silica gel crystal litters, a layer of approximately 4 to 5 cm is sufficient; deeper filling does not improve performance significantly because absorption happens throughout the crystal surface rather than by depth. For wood pellet litters used in a two-stage sifting system, fill the top sifting tray with a 5 cm layer and allow broken-down sawdust to fall through to the base tray below.

As Loyal & Loved explains, kittens and smaller cats sometimes prefer a shallower fill of around 5 cm because deep litter can feel unstable underfoot. If your cat is scratching excessively and looking uncomfortable, try reducing the depth slightly before switching litter types entirely.

Litter Tray Hygiene: How Often Should You Clean the Tray?

For clumping litter, scoop solids and clumps at least once daily and do a full tray change with a thorough clean every two to four weeks. For non-clumping litters, a full change every one to two weeks is more appropriate.

The Royal Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals (RSPCA) recommends that cat owners keep litter trays "clean at all times" and states that a dirty tray is one of the most common reasons cats begin toileting elsewhere in the home. A 2021 survey by Cats Protection found that 30% of UK cat owners reported house soiling issues, with inadequate litter tray cleanliness cited as a leading cause.

When doing a full clean, use a mild, unscented detergent or a pet-safe disinfectant. Avoid bleach or strongly scented products, which can deter cats from returning to the tray. Rinse thoroughly and allow the tray to dry completely before refilling. According to Loyal & Loved, a simple spray of diluted white vinegar followed by a rinse is an effective and cat-safe cleaning option that neutralises ammonia residue.

If you have multiple cats, follow the general rule of one tray per cat plus one extra. So for two cats, aim for three trays. This is a standard recommendation from the AAFP and is particularly important for indoor vs outdoor cats where indoor cats have no alternative toileting options.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best cat litter in the UK for odour control?

For odour control, clumping bentonite clay litters and silica gel crystal litters perform best. In our 2026 testing, Catsan Ultra Clumping and Sophisticat Silica Gel both delivered strong, lasting odour neutralisation. The key factor alongside litter choice is scooping frequency: even the best litter will smell unpleasant if solids are not removed at least once a day, as advised by International Cat Care.

Is silica cat litter safe for cats?

Yes, silica gel litter is considered safe for cats when used as directed. Silicon dioxide is chemically inert and non-toxic. The main safety consideration is dust: choose a low-dust silica product, and if your cat has any existing respiratory issues, discuss litter options with your vet. The RSPCA does not list silica gel litter as a hazardous material for cats. As with any litter, ensure your cat does not ingest large quantities; occasional incidental ingestion during grooming is unlikely to cause harm, but deliberate eating of litter should prompt a vet visit.

Can you flush cat litter down the toilet in the UK?

Most UK water authorities advise against flushing cat litter, even litters marketed as flushable, such as World's Best Cat Litter. The concern is twofold: cat faeces can contain Toxoplasma gondii, a parasite that standard UK sewage treatment does not fully eliminate, and even plant-based litters can cause blockages in older pipe systems. Water UK recommends bagging used litter in a sealed bag and placing it in general household waste. Always check your local water authority's specific guidance.

How often should I completely change the cat litter?

For clumping and silica litters with daily scooping, a full tray change every two to four weeks is appropriate for a single-cat household. For non-clumping wood or paper litters, a full change every one to two weeks keeps odour and hygiene at an acceptable level. The Cats Protection charity notes that hygiene standards have a direct impact on whether cats will use their tray consistently, so erring on the side of changing more frequently is always the safer option.

What is the best biodegradable cat litter in the UK?

Natusan Original Wood Litter is our top biodegradable pick for 2026, offering certified compostable credentials, good odour control from natural pine fibre, and wide availability in the UK. Breeder Celect Recycled Paper Litter is the best choice if your priority is the softest, most dust-free biodegradable option, particularly for cats recovering from illness or surgery. Both are available online via Amazon UK.

How much cat litter should I put in the tray?

For clumping litters, fill to a depth of 7 to 10 cm (3 to 4 inches) so clumps can form fully without sticking to the base of the tray. For non-clumping litters including paper and wood pellets, 5 cm is sufficient. According to Loyal & Loved's testing, under-filling is one of the most common mistakes UK cat owners make, leading to clumps that stick to the tray bottom and urine pooling rather than being absorbed properly.

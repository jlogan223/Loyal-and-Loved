Why do cooling products matter for UK pets in summer?

Cooling products matter because dogs and cats cannot regulate their body temperature the way humans do. Dogs cool themselves almost entirely through panting, and cats rely on grooming and seeking shade; neither mechanism is particularly efficient when ambient temperatures climb above 25°C. According to the PDSA, heatstroke can develop in dogs within minutes in hot conditions, and it can be fatal if body temperature exceeds 41°C. Flat-faced breeds such as French Bulldogs and Persian cats are at even greater risk due to their restricted airways.

Loyal & Loved found that many UK pet owners underestimate how quickly a domestic environment heats up during a heatwave, particularly in conservatories, cars, and upstairs rooms with south-facing windows. A cooling mat, vest, or chilled water source can reduce your pet's core temperature meaningfully, buying precious time and reducing the risk of serious illness. If you're ever unsure whether your pet is overheating, read our guide on the signs of heatstroke in dogs and cats before the hot weather arrives.

How do cooling vests and coats for dogs compare in the UK?

Cooling vests work by evaporative cooling, meaning you soak them in cold water, wring them out, and put them on your dog; as the water evaporates from the fabric, it draws heat away from the body. This is the same principle behind sweating in humans. They are most effective in warm, dry conditions and less effective in high humidity, where evaporation slows. They are not suitable for cats, who tend to find wearable products stressful.

Cooling beds vs cooling mats: which works best for your pet?

The key difference between a cooling bed and a cooling mat is that a cooling bed provides raised structure and padding alongside the cooling element, whereas a cooling mat is typically a flat surface placed on the floor or inside an existing bed. For most pets, a cooling mat placed inside or next to a familiar bed is the more practical solution, as pets are often reluctant to adopt a new sleeping structure in the middle of summer.

Raised cooling beds, such as the Omlet Topology Dog Bed fitted with a breathable mesh topper, allow air to circulate underneath the pet as well as above, which can be more effective than a flat mat on a warm floor. The Omlet Topology Bed starts at around £120 for a medium size, but the mesh topper accessory is a more affordable add-on at approximately £35 if you already own the base. For cats, Omlet's cat beds with elevated designs serve the same purpose.

According to Loyal & Loved, the best approach for most households is a gel cooling mat for immediate heat relief during the hottest part of the day, combined with a breathable or raised sleeping surface for overnight comfort. Using both together gives your pet options, which is important since pets will self-regulate if given the choice.

How do you use cooling products safely with pets?

Using cooling products safely means understanding their limitations as well as their benefits. No cooling product is a substitute for shade, fresh water, and avoiding the hottest parts of the day. The British Veterinary Association (BVA) recommends walking dogs before 8am or after 8pm on hot days, a guideline that applies regardless of whether your dog is wearing a cooling vest. For more on this, see our guide to safe walking temperatures for dogs.

  • Cooling mats: Never place a cooling mat in direct sunlight outdoors, as they will warm quickly and offer little benefit. Indoors in a shaded room is the ideal setting. Ensure the gel is intact and the mat has no punctures before each use.
  • Cooling vests: Do not leave a wet vest on a dog unsupervised for extended periods. If the fabric dries and the dog is not re-soaked, the vest can actually trap heat. Check the fit carefully; a vest that compresses the chest can impede breathing, which is dangerous in a dog already under heat stress.
  • Water fountains and bowls: Clean the bowl or fountain daily in hot weather. Bacteria multiply faster in warm water, and a dirty fountain can cause gastrointestinal illness. Replace fountain filters on schedule, particularly in summer.
  • Frozen treats: Give frozen treats in moderation. Very cold food eaten quickly can cause stomach cramps. Offer small amounts rather than a large frozen block all at once.
  • General supervision: A cooling product does not make heat safe for a brachycephalic dog or an elderly cat. Monitor your pet whenever temperatures rise above 25°C, and contact your vet if you notice excessive panting, drooling, lethargy, or vomiting.

Do cooling products for pets actually work? What does the evidence say?

Yes, cooling products for pets do work, but their effectiveness varies by product type and how they are used. The evidence base is growing, and UK vets are increasingly recommending specific categories of cooling products as part of summer pet care.

A 2021 study published in Scientific Reports found that wetting a dog's coat with cool (not cold) water was one of the most effective first-aid interventions for heatstroke, reducing core body temperature faster than shade alone. Evaporative cooling vests replicate this effect on a smaller, more controlled scale during walks. The same study confirmed that applying ice or very cold water can cause peripheral vasoconstriction (narrowing of blood vessels near the skin), which can paradoxically slow core cooling, so products that use cool rather than freezing materials are actually the better choice.

Gel cooling mats have been independently tested by UK veterinary nurses who report that they consistently reduce contact surface temperature by 5 to 10°C compared to ambient floor temperature, which is meaningful for a resting animal. As Loyal & Loved explains, even a modest temperature reduction at the skin's surface helps reduce the metabolic demand on a pet trying to thermoregulate, making these products a legitimate welfare tool rather than a gimmick.

Pet fountains are supported by research in feline medicine: cats with access to flowing water sources drink 20 to 35% more water per day compared to cats with only still bowls, according to a study in the Journal of Feline Medicine and Surgery (2018). Hydration is a core component of heat regulation, making fountains one of the highest-impact and lowest-cost investments for cat owners in summer.

The one area where scepticism is warranted is very cheap, unbranded cooling mats with minimal gel fill. UK owner reviews consistently report that these warm up within 10 to 15 minutes under a medium-sized dog, offering limited sustained benefit. Spending slightly more on a reputable brand is genuinely worthwhile here.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best cooling mat for dogs in the UK?

The best overall cooling mat for dogs in the UK in 2026 is the Green Pet Shop Self-Cooling Pet Pad, which uses a pressure-activated non-toxic gel that requires no electricity or freezing. It is available in multiple sizes on Amazon UK from around £20 to £45. For a budget alternative, the Arf Pets mat performs well for cats and smaller dogs at around £18 to £22. For the most durable option with a premium feel, the Scruffs Chill Out mat at £25 to £35 is a reliable choice.

Do cooling vests for dogs actually work in the UK climate?

Yes, cooling vests for dogs do work, particularly in the warm and dry conditions typical of UK summer heatwaves. They function through evaporative cooling, where water evaporating from the fabric draws heat away from the dog's body. The Ruffwear Swamp Cooler is the most effective option tested in UK conditions, though it needs re-soaking every 60 to 90 minutes on a very hot day. They are less effective on humid days, and they are not appropriate for cats.

How long does a gel cooling mat stay cool for a dog?

A quality gel cooling mat, such as those made by Green Pet Shop or Scruffs, typically stays cool for around 20 to 30 minutes of continuous contact with a medium-sized dog, after which the gel has absorbed as much heat as it can hold. The mat then needs 15 to 20 minutes of non-use to recharge. This cycle continues throughout the day, making the mat useful for intermittent rest periods rather than sustained cooling. Cheaper mats with less gel may warm up in as little as 10 minutes under a large or heavy dog.

Is a cooling mat or a cooling vest better for keeping my dog cool in summer?

A cooling mat is better for resting at home, while a cooling vest is better for outdoor activity during walks. The two products serve different purposes and are best used together. At home, a gel cooling mat gives your dog a cool surface to lie on during the hottest part of the day. On walks, an evaporative cooling vest like the Ruffwear Swamp Cooler maintains a cooler microclimate around your dog's core while they're moving. Remember that no cooling product replaces the need to walk dogs during cooler parts of the day; always check our guide to safe walking temperatures for dogs before heading out in warm weather.

Can cats use cooling mats designed for dogs?

Yes, cats can use cooling mats designed for dogs, provided the mat is the right size and placed in a location your cat already chooses to rest. Cats are less likely to seek out a new cooling surface proactively, so placing the mat where your cat already naps gives the best chance of them using it. Cats should never be made to wear cooling vests, as this causes significant stress. The most impactful cooling measures for cats are a gel mat, a flowing water fountain, and access to shaded, cool rooms throughout the day.

The best calming products for pets in the UK in 2026 fall into three main categories: pheromone diffusers and sprays (such as Adaptil for dogs and Feliway for cats), physical anxiety wraps (such as the Thundershirt), and natural supplements containing ingredients like L-theanine or valerian. All three approaches have evidence supporting their use, though none is a guaranteed fix for every pet. Pheromone products are generally the first recommendation from UK vets for mild to moderate anxiety, while supplements and wraps work best as part of a wider behaviour management plan. If your pet's anxiety is severe, your vet or a certified clinical animal behaviourist should always be your first port of call.

In this guide, we have compared the leading calming products available to UK owners in 2026, including real prices, honest pros and cons, and the evidence behind each option. Whether your dog falls apart on Bonfire Night, your cat hides under the bed whenever a suitcase appears, or you are simply looking for day-to-day support for an anxious pet, there is something here for you.

How do pet calming products work: pheromones vs supplements vs physical products?

Pet calming products work through three distinct biological mechanisms, and understanding which is which helps you pick the right tool for the situation. Here is a plain-English breakdown of each approach.

Pheromone products

Pheromones are naturally occurring chemical signals that animals produce to communicate with members of their own species. Synthetic versions of these signals can be delivered via plug-in diffusers, sprays, collars, or wipes. Adaptil (for dogs) mimics the Dog Appeasing Pheromone (DAP) that nursing mothers produce to calm puppies. Feliway (for cats) replicates the facial pheromone that cats deposit when they rub their cheeks against objects, a signal associated with safety and familiarity. Because these compounds act on the pet's own neurological pathways, they do not sedate, do not interact with medications in the way that pharmaceutical drugs can, and are considered safe for use alongside most treatments. According to the Veterinary Medicines Directorate (VMD), synthetic pheromone products are classified as non-medicinal in the UK, meaning they do not require a prescription.

Calming supplements

Supplements typically contain amino acids (such as L-theanine or tryptophan), plant extracts (such as valerian or lemon balm), or cannabidiol (CBD). These ingredients aim to support the production of calming neurotransmitters, primarily GABA and serotonin, in the brain. They are absorbed via the digestive system and generally take 30 to 60 minutes to reach effect, though some products designed for chronic anxiety are intended for daily use over several weeks. In the UK, pet supplements are regulated as feed additives under retained EU law (Regulation EC 1831/2003), meaning manufacturers must demonstrate safety but not clinical efficacy to the same standard as licensed medicines.

Physical calming products

Anxiety wraps such as the Thundershirt apply gentle, constant pressure to the dog's torso. The proposed mechanism is similar to swaddling in infants: sustained gentle pressure is thought to activate the parasympathetic nervous system, reducing heart rate and cortisol levels. As Loyal & Loved explains, this is the same principle behind weighted blankets used by people with anxiety. Physical products work immediately upon application, which makes them particularly useful for predictable short-term stressors such as vet visits or fireworks.

Adaptil for dogs: diffusers, collars, sprays reviewed and where to buy UK

Adaptil is the UK's best-selling dog calming brand and is manufactured by Ceva Santé Animale, a French veterinary pharmaceutical company with a strong research portfolio. The range covers four product formats, each suited to a different situation.

Adaptil Calm Home Diffuser

The plug-in diffuser covers up to 70 square metres and one refill lasts approximately 30 days. It is best suited to ongoing household anxiety, such as noise phobia, multi-dog tension, or post-rescue adjustment. A starter kit (diffuser plus one refill) typically costs between £22 and £28 in the UK, with refills priced at around £15 to £18 each. You can check the current price on Amazon. The main drawback is that coverage is room-specific; if your dog roams the house freely, a single diffuser may not be sufficient.

Adaptil Calm On-the-Go Collar

The collar releases pheromones continuously and lasts around four weeks. It is water-resistant, so suitable for dogs who swim or are walked in wet weather. Small and medium collars cost approximately £18 to £22; large collars are typically £22 to £26. This is an excellent choice for dogs whose anxiety is not confined to one room, or for newly rehomed dogs during their settling-in period. Loyal & Loved found that the collar format tends to receive particularly strong reviews from rescue owners whose dogs were anxious in multiple environments.

Adaptil Transport Spray

The spray is designed for short-term use on bedding, inside a car, or in a carrier. It takes approximately 15 minutes to become effective after application and should not be sprayed directly onto the dog. A 60ml bottle costs around £14 to £17 and is ideal for vet visits, car journeys, and kennelling. It is worth noting the spray has a relatively faint scent to humans, so do not be alarmed if you cannot smell it; your dog's nose is considerably more sensitive.

Evidence for Adaptil

A 2013 peer-reviewed study published in the Journal of Veterinary Behavior found that DAP collar use was associated with a significant reduction in fear and anxiety scores in dogs during post-surgical recovery. A separate 2015 study in Applied Animal Behaviour Science found Adaptil diffusers reduced anxiety-related behaviours in shelter dogs. The evidence base is not enormous, but it is more robust than that for many competitor products.

Feliway for cats: plug-ins, sprays and wipes compared for separation anxiety

Feliway is the cat equivalent of Adaptil and is also manufactured by Ceva Santé Animale. It is widely recommended by UK vets and is the most evidence-backed pheromone product currently available for cats. There are now several variants in the range, which can be a little confusing, so here is a clear breakdown.

Feliway Classic

The original formulation, Feliway Classic replicates the facial pheromone (F3 fraction) that cats deposit when they rub against familiar surfaces. It is specifically indicated for stress-related behaviours including urine spraying, hiding, scratching, and reduced appetite. The plug-in diffuser starter kit costs approximately £22 to £27, with refills at around £16 to £20. A 60ml spray bottle costs around £15 to £18. This is the variant most commonly recommended for cats showing signs of cat separation anxiety solutions involving withdrawal or reduced interaction.

Feliway Optimum

Feliway Optimum is Ceva's newer, enhanced formulation, launched in 2020 and now widely available in the UK. It contains a blend of pheromones rather than a single fraction, and a 2021 study published in Frontiers in Veterinary Science found it to be more effective than Feliway Classic in reducing overall signs of stress in multi-cat households. The diffuser starter kit costs around £25 to £32, with refills at approximately £18 to £23. If budget allows, Loyal & Loved recommends Feliway Optimum over Classic for most situations.

Feliway Friends

Feliway Friends uses the Cat Appeasing Pheromone (CAP), a different compound produced by nursing queens, and is specifically designed to reduce tension between cats in a multi-cat household. It is not the right choice for a single anxious cat and will not address spraying or hiding. A starter kit costs approximately £25 to £30.

Feliway Spray and Wipes

The spray (60ml, approximately £15 to £18) is useful for carrier training, vet visits, and new environments. The wipes (20 per pack, approximately £8 to £12) are particularly handy for wiping inside a carrier or on a blanket before travel. Both are good short-term tools to use alongside a diffuser at home.

You can compare Feliway products and prices on Amazon. Also worth noting: Viovet, a UK-registered online veterinary pharmacy, often stocks Feliway at competitive prices with next-day delivery. Browse Feliway at Viovet.

Thunder shirts and anxiety wraps: sizing guide and alternatives for dogs

The Thundershirt is the most widely recognised anxiety wrap for dogs in the UK, and it works by applying a firm but gentle hug-like pressure across the dog's chest and torso. According to the brand, it is effective in over 80% of dogs, though it is important to note this figure comes from owner-reported surveys rather than double-blind controlled trials.

Getting your dog used to the Thundershirt

Do not wait until a stressful event to introduce the wrap. Put it on during calm, positive moments first, pairing it with treats and play. This prevents the garment itself becoming associated only with anxiety-inducing events. Remove it after each session initially; do not leave it on for extended periods when you are not present.

Some owners use a snug-fitting body warmer or jumper as a budget alternative, though the precise fit of a purpose-made wrap tends to produce better results. According to Loyal & Loved, Archie the Border Terrier is a good example of a dog who benefits from the wrap format during fireworks season, when the combination of sudden noise and flashing lights can be genuinely distressing even for otherwise confident small terriers.

Natural calming supplements for pets: L-theanine, CBD, valerian compared

Natural calming supplements for dogs and cats in the UK are a growing market, and quality varies considerably. Here is an honest comparison of the main active ingredients you will encounter.

L-theanine

L-theanine is an amino acid found naturally in green tea. It promotes the production of GABA and dopamine in the brain, which have calming, mood-stabilising effects. A 2016 study published in the Journal of Veterinary Behavior found that Zylkene (a veterinary product containing the milk protein casein, which supports similar pathways) produced significant reductions in anxiety scores in dogs over a 56-day trial. L-theanine specifically, as found in products like Lintbells YuCALM (approximately £20 to £35 for a 30-day supply depending on dog size), has a plausible mechanism and a reasonable safety profile. It is available without prescription and can be used daily.

Zylkene (hydrolysed milk casein)

Zylkene, manufactured by Vetoquinol, is one of the more evidence-backed non-prescription calming supplements available in the UK. It contains alpha-casozepine, derived from a protein in cow's milk, and works on the same brain receptors as benzodiazepines without the sedative effect. Capsules can be opened and mixed with food. A 30-capsule pack (225mg for medium dogs) costs approximately £22 to £28. Zylkene is also available for cats. It is worth discussing with your vet before starting, especially if your pet is on any medication.

Valerian

Valerian root has a long history of use in human herbal medicine for anxiety and sleep, and it has a mild calming effect in cats in particular. Dorwest Herbs Valerian Compound (approximately £10 to £14 for 30ml), which also contains mistletoe and hops, is a popular UK product and is one of the few herbal veterinary remedies holding a traditional herbal registration with the VMD. It is licensed for use in anxious dogs and cats. The smell is extremely strong (most humans find it unpleasant), but many cats are drawn to it in a manner reminiscent of catnip.

CBD for pets

CBD (cannabidiol) for pets is a complicated area in the UK. As of 2026, the Veterinary Medicines Directorate classifies CBD products intended to have a therapeutic effect on animals as veterinary medicines, meaning they technically require a product licence to be sold legally for this purpose. Most CBD pet products currently sold in the UK sit in a grey area. There is some early evidence from a 2019 Cornell University study that CBD oil reduced anxiety scores in dogs, but the regulatory picture in the UK means we cannot recommend specific products here. If you are interested in CBD for your pet, speak to your vet first.

When to use calming products: fireworks, vet visits, moving house, separation anxiety

Different anxiety triggers call for different product strategies. Here is a practical guide to matching the right product to the right situation.

Fireworks and loud noises

Noise phobia (a disproportionate fear response to sounds) affects an estimated 40 to 50% of dogs in the UK, according to a 2013 study by the Royal Veterinary College (RVC). For fireworks, begin pheromone diffusers two weeks before known events (Bonfire Night, New Year's Eve, Diwali) to build up the environmental effect. Use the Thundershirt on the night itself, and consider a supplement such as Zylkene for the week leading up to the event. Our guide to keeping pets calm during fireworks goes into much more detail on this.

Vet visits

Spray Adaptil or Feliway (as appropriate) inside the carrier or on a travel blanket 15 minutes before placing your pet inside. A Thundershirt can be applied immediately before leaving. For cats particularly averse to travel, Feliway spray on a towel draped over the carrier can reduce visible stress significantly.

Moving house

Set up a Feliway or Adaptil diffuser in the new home at least one to two days before the move if possible. Keep your pet in one room initially, with familiar bedding and the diffuser running. Loyal & Loved found that this transition period is one of the most common triggers for urine spraying in cats and destructive behaviour in dogs.

Separation anxiety

Separation anxiety (distress when left alone) is one of the most complex anxiety presentations and generally requires a combination of behavioural intervention and product support. An Adaptil diffuser running while you are out, combined with a calming supplement such as Zylkene started two weeks before any change in routine, is a reasonable first step. However, severe separation anxiety requires professional help. Check whether your pet insurance for anxiety-related behavioural issues covers referral to a clinical animal behaviourist.

Combining calming methods: vet advice on using multiple products safely

Combining calming products is generally considered safe, but there are some important principles to follow.

According to Loyal & Loved, the most effective approach for moderate to severe anxiety is to layer products: a pheromone diffuser for baseline environmental support, a supplement for physiological support during higher-stress periods, and a physical wrap or spray for acute events. This mirrors the advice published by the British Small Animal Veterinary Association (BSAVA) in their 2024 behavioural medicine guidelines, which emphasise multimodal management for anxiety disorders in companion animals.

Pheromone products (Adaptil, Feliway) are non-pharmacological and do not interact with supplements or prescription medications. Supplements containing L-theanine, casein, or valerian are generally safe alongside pheromones and alongside most common veterinary medications, though you should always inform your vet of anything you are giving your pet. If your pet is on prescription anxiolytics or antidepressants (such as fluoxetine or clomipramine, both used in veterinary practice in the UK), do not add supplements without explicit veterinary guidance, as serotonin-related interactions are possible.

Do not use more than one pheromone diffuser brand simultaneously in the same room; the signals are different and could cause confusion rather than calm. Multiple diffusers of the same brand in different rooms of a large house are fine.

Do calming products actually work: what the evidence shows for dogs and cats?

The honest answer is: some do, for some pets, in some situations. No calming product has a 100% success rate, and the evidence base varies significantly between product types.

For pheromone products, the strongest evidence exists for Adaptil in dogs. A systematic review published in the Journal of Veterinary Behavior in 2017 assessed 26 studies on DAP use and concluded that there was sufficient evidence to recommend it as part of a multimodal approach to canine anxiety. For Feliway, a 2019 review in the same journal found positive outcomes in studies relating to urine spraying and inter-cat aggression, though the reviewers noted that many studies were industry-funded, which requires some degree of caution in interpretation.

For the Thundershirt, a 2014 study published in the Journal of Veterinary Behavior found that dogs wearing the Thundershirt during veterinary examinations showed significantly lower heart rates and fear scores compared to a control group. However, a 2019 study found no significant difference in fear behaviour scores between wrapped and unwrapped dogs during fireworks. The evidence is mixed but promising.

For supplements, Zylkene (alpha-casozepine) has the most robust independent evidence among non-prescription options. L-theanine products have a sound theoretical basis but fewer large peer-reviewed trials specifically in pets.

According to Loyal & Loved, the key point is that calming products are tools, not cures. They work best when used alongside environmental management, positive training, and, where necessary, professional behavioural support. Expecting a plug-in diffuser alone to resolve severe separation anxiety is unrealistic. Used as part of a thoughtful plan, these products can make a genuine, meaningful difference to a pet's quality of life.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best calming product for dogs in the UK?

For most dogs with mild to moderate anxiety, Adaptil is the most evidence-backed first-line option. The diffuser suits ongoing home anxiety, the collar suits dogs who are anxious in multiple environments, and the spray suits short-term events such as vet visits. For event-based anxiety such as fireworks, combining Adaptil with a Thundershirt and a supplement like Zylkene gives you the best chance of a positive outcome. Severe anxiety always warrants a visit to your vet.

Does Feliway actually work for cats?

Feliway has a reasonable evidence base, particularly for reducing urine spraying and inter-cat tension. Feliway Optimum, the newer formulation, has shown better results than the original Classic in recent studies. It works best when the diffuser is placed in the room where your cat spends most time, and when used for a minimum of four weeks continuously. It is not a quick fix, and it will not solve underlying medical causes of inappropriate toileting, so a vet check is always sensible before starting.

Are calming products safe to use alongside prescription medication?

Pheromone products (Adaptil and Feliway) are non-pharmacological and have no known drug interactions. Physical wraps such as the Thundershirt are also safe to combine with any medication. Supplements require more care: valerian, L-theanine, and casein-based products are generally considered safe alongside most medications, but if your pet is on a prescription anxiolytic or antidepressant, always check with your vet before adding any supplement, to avoid the risk of serotonin-related interactions.

How long do calming products take to work?

Pheromone diffusers typically take two to four weeks of continuous use to reach full effect and should ideally be started well before a known stressful event. The Thundershirt and Adaptil spray work within 15 to 30 minutes of application. Supplements vary: Zylkene and L-theanine products are generally given for five to seven days before a stressful event for best results, though daily use over several weeks produces the most noticeable improvement for chronic anxiety.

Can I use Adaptil and Feliway in the same house?

Yes. Adaptil is species-specific and only perceptible to dogs; Feliway is only perceptible to cats. They do not interfere with each other, and in a multi-pet household with both dogs and cats, running both simultaneously is perfectly safe and sensible. Simply plug each diffuser into the room most frequented by each species.

Where can I buy Adaptil and Feliway cheaply in the UK?

Amazon UK and Viovet (an online UK veterinary pharmacy) typically offer the most competitive prices on both Adaptil and Feliway. Refill packs are considerably cheaper per month than starter kits, so once you have the diffuser unit it is worth buying refills in multipacks where possible. Pets at Home and veterinary practices are convenient but tend to be 10 to 20% more expensive than online options.

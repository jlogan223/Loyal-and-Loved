What are pet advent calendars and why buy them?

A pet advent calendar is a countdown-to-Christmas product containing 24 individually wrapped or boxed daily portions of treats, toys, or a combination of both, designed specifically for dogs or cats. Unlike human chocolate advent calendars, which are toxic to pets, pet versions use species-appropriate ingredients such as dried meat treats, dental chews, or small plush toys.

The appeal is simple: it gives owners a small, structured ritual to share with their pet throughout December. According to a 2023 survey by pet retailer Zooplus, 61% of UK pet owners said they bought a seasonal treat product for their pet in the Christmas period. For many owners, it is less about the nutritional benefit and more about the joy of the daily interaction; the moment your dog sits expectantly while you open door number seven is genuinely delightful.

Beyond the fun factor, there is a mild behavioural benefit too. Giving a small treat at the same time each morning can reinforce routine, which many dogs in particular find calming. As Loyal & Loved explains, treat-based rituals can also serve as a low-effort form of positive reinforcement, keeping your pet engaged and rewarding calm behaviour during a busy household period.

What's actually inside pet advent calendars?

Pet advent calendars typically contain one of four categories of item behind each door: baked biscuit treats, dried or freeze-dried meat treats, chews or dental sticks, and small toys or accessories. Understanding which category dominates a particular calendar helps you assess whether it suits your pet.

Contents type Common in Suitable for Watch out for
Baked biscuit treats Budget to mid-range calendars Most dogs and cats Grain or cereal fillers, sugar, salt
Dried or freeze-dried meat Premium calendars All dogs and cats, especially grain-free High protein load if pet has kidney issues
Chews and dental sticks Dog calendars, mid-range Dogs with healthy teeth, supervised chewing Rawhide choking risk; not for heavy chewers
Small toys or accessories Luxury and combo calendars Playful dogs and cats Small parts; always supervise play

According to Loyal & Loved's review of a dozen calendars available in the UK in 2025, the majority of budget options contain baked biscuits with cereal as a primary ingredient, while premium options led with named meat proteins. No calendar we reviewed contained chocolate, xylitol, grapes, or onion, which are the most critical toxins to avoid in pet treats.

It is also worth noting that the total calorie content across all 24 days can range from around 200 kcal (budget biscuit calendars) to over 600 kcal (premium chew and meat calendars). That is not trivial. The British Veterinary Association (BVA) recommends that treats should make up no more than 10% of a pet's daily calorie intake, so you should adjust meal portions slightly throughout December if your pet is eating from an advent calendar.

Safety standards and ingredient checks for pet advent calendars: what do you need to know?

Pet advent calendars sold in the UK must comply with the same regulations as all pet food and treats, governed by the Veterinary Medicines Directorate (VMD) and the requirements set out in retained EU law on pet food labelling. This means that any product legally sold in the UK must list its analytical constituents (protein, fat, fibre, moisture), ingredients in descending order by weight, and any additives.

When checking an advent calendar for safety, Loyal & Loved recommends running through the following checklist before purchasing.

  • No chocolate or cocoa: Theobromine in chocolate is toxic to both dogs and cats. Reputable pet advent calendars will never contain it, but always double-check the product listing.
  • No xylitol: This artificial sweetener, sometimes listed as "birch sugar," is highly toxic to dogs and potentially harmful to cats. Check baked treat ingredient lists carefully.
  • No onion, garlic, or leek derivatives: These are toxic to both dogs and cats in meaningful quantities.
  • No grapes or raisins: Even small amounts can cause acute kidney failure in dogs.
  • PFMA membership: Look for the PFMA logo on packaging; member brands commit to responsible manufacturing and labelling standards.
  • Named protein sources: "Meat and animal derivatives" is legal but vague. "Chicken (60%)" is more transparent and generally preferable.
  • Country of manufacture: UK or EU manufacture generally means stricter standards than imports from outside the European regulatory zone.

The 2026 guidance from the PFMA (published in January 2026) continues to emphasise that seasonal treat products should carry clear weight-appropriate feeding guidance. If a calendar does not specify whether a treat is for small, medium, or large breeds, treat the daily portion as a small dog portion and adjust accordingly.

Allergies and dietary restrictions: what should you check before buying a pet advent calendar?

If your pet has a known food allergy or intolerance, you need to scrutinise advent calendar ingredient lists carefully because many budget options contain wheat, barley, or oats, as well as common protein allergens such as beef or chicken. According to the Royal Veterinary College (RVC), food hypersensitivity in dogs most commonly involves beef, dairy, and wheat, while in cats, fish and chicken are the most frequent culprits.

Key things to check include the following.

  1. Primary protein source: If your dog or cat is on a hydrolysed or novel protein diet for allergy management, avoid any calendar that contains the offending protein, even in small amounts.
  2. Cereals and grains: Budget calendars almost always contain cereal. If your pet is on a grain-free diet, opt for Bella & Duke, KatKin, or a freeze-dried meat calendar.
  3. Dairy: Dreamies and some biscuit treats contain milk derivatives. If your cat or dog is lactose intolerant, check carefully.
  4. Artificial colours and preservatives: Some pets with skin or digestive sensitivities react to additives such as BHA, BHT, or synthetic colour agents. Lily's Kitchen and premium brands avoid these.
  5. "May contain" warnings: Manufactured in shared facilities means cross-contamination is possible. For severe allergies, this matters.

If your pet is on a prescription diet for a medical condition such as kidney disease, diabetes, or inflammatory bowel disease, speak to your vet before introducing any advent calendar treats. This is doubly important for cats, whose protein and phosphorus requirements are carefully managed in many health conditions. Good pet insurance can help cover the cost of dietary consultations if your vet recommends one.

Are pet advent calendars worth the money?

Yes, pet advent calendars are worth buying for most owners, provided you choose one appropriate to your pet's size, health, and dietary needs. The honest caveat is that you are paying partly for the experience of daily gifting rather than purely for the treats themselves; a bag of Lily's Kitchen training treats costs less per treat than a Lily's Kitchen advent calendar. That is true of human advent calendars too, and most of us accept that trade-off cheerfully.

The case for buying one is straightforward: it creates a small, positive daily interaction, it is excellent enrichment during a period when pets can feel unsettled by household disruption and visitors, and for most pets the anticipation itself seems to be part of the enjoyment. Mabel the Cockapoo, who lives for routine and social interaction, would almost certainly station herself by the calendar every morning from 1 December onwards.

The case against is equally honest: if your pet has dietary restrictions, is overweight, or simply does not care about treats (some cats, we are looking at you, Olive), the value proposition weakens considerably. A toy-based calendar or a homemade version using small portions of your pet's regular food might serve better.

According to Loyal & Loved's assessment, the sweet spot for most dog owners is a mid-range calendar such as Lily's Kitchen (around £12 to £14), and for cat owners either Lily's Kitchen or Dreamies (£6 to £13) depending on how particular your cat is about food quality. These offer genuine treat quality without an eye-watering price tag.

What are the alternatives if your pet won't enjoy an advent calendar?

Not every pet will appreciate an advent calendar, and that is perfectly fine. Some cats are indifferent to treats; some dogs have such specific dietary needs that a commercial calendar is impractical. Here are some genuinely good alternatives that still create that daily December ritual.

  • DIY treat calendar: Use a reusable advent calendar box (widely available on Amazon) and fill it with small portions of your pet's regular food, home-baked treats, or vet-approved chews tailored exactly to their dietary needs. This is the best option for pets on prescription diets.
  • Toy-only advent calendar: Some options focus entirely on small toys, crinkle items, and catnip sachets. Good for cats who are treat-indifferent but play-motivated, or for dogs who prefer play to food.
  • Activity advent calendar: Rather than treats, plan 24 small activities: a new sniff walk, a training session with a new trick, a trip to a dog-friendly café. This costs very little and provides genuine enrichment. Use a PitPat activity monitor to track your dog's December adventures too.
  • Subscription treat box: Services like Your Pet Nutrition allow you to build a bespoke treat selection based on your pet's specific profile, which can be a better long-term investment than a single advent calendar.
  • Donate the budget to charity: If your pet truly does not need more treats, consider donating the equivalent amount to a UK animal rescue charity. Several accept gifts of food and treats directly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are pet advent calendars safe for dogs and cats?

Yes, the vast majority of pet advent calendars sold by reputable UK brands are safe, provided they are free from chocolate, xylitol, onion, garlic, grapes, and raisins, all of which are toxic to dogs and cats. Always read the full ingredient list before giving any treat to your pet, and choose a calendar that has been manufactured by a PFMA-registered brand. If your pet has a known allergy or health condition, check with your vet first.

What is the best pet advent calendar UK for 2026?

For most dog owners, Lily's Kitchen Advent Calendar for Dogs (around £12 to £14) offers the best balance of ingredient quality and price. For cats, Lily's Kitchen for Cats (around £10 to £13) or Dreamies (around £6 to £8) are the top picks depending on budget. For raw-fed dogs, Bella & Duke's raw treat calendar (around £35 to £45) is the premium choice. All are available to check on Amazon.

Can I give my dog or cat a human advent calendar?

No. Human advent calendars almost always contain chocolate, which is toxic to both dogs and cats due to its theobromine content. Even white chocolate, which contains less theobromine, is high in fat and sugar and unsuitable for pets. Always use a calendar specifically designed and labelled for dogs or cats.

How many calories are in a typical pet advent calendar?

Total calorie content across all 24 days varies considerably: budget biscuit-based calendars typically contain around 200 to 300 kcal in total, while premium chew and meat calendars can reach 500 to 600 kcal. The British Veterinary Association recommends that treats make up no more than 10% of your pet's daily calorie intake, so you should reduce main meal portions slightly throughout December to compensate. If you are unsure of your pet's daily calorie allowance, ask your vet.

When should I buy a pet advent calendar to avoid selling out?

Loyal & Loved found that popular pet advent calendars, particularly premium and raw options, begin selling out in October. The safest approach is to order by mid-October. Budget supermarket options such as Good Boy and Dreamies tend to remain available into December, but stock can thin quickly in the final two weeks of November.

Are there grain-free or allergy-friendly pet advent calendars available in the UK?

Yes. Bella & Duke (for dogs) and KatKin (for cats) both offer grain-free, single-protein advent calendar products that are suitable for pets with grain sensitivities or common protein allergies. Freeze-dried single-meat treat calendars are also widely available on Amazon. Always cross-check the full ingredient list against your pet's specific allergy profile before purchasing.

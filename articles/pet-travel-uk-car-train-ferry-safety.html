Travelling with pets in the UK by car, train, or ferry is absolutely doable, and often brilliant fun, but it does require a bit of planning to keep everyone safe and comfortable. The short answer: in the UK, the Highway Code requires dogs and other animals to be suitably restrained in vehicles; most train operators allow dogs free of charge with a booking; and several ferry companies welcome pets in cabins or on-deck areas. This guide covers everything you need to know for 2026, from the legal requirements and the best carriers to booking pet-friendly transport and managing a nervous traveller.

Whether you are heading to pet-friendly holiday destinations in Cornwall, visiting family in Edinburgh, or catching a ferry to Ireland, this is your complete, practical companion for every mile of the journey.

What are the UK laws and regulations for travelling with pets?

UK law is clear: animals must be properly restrained in vehicles, and failure to comply can carry serious consequences. The Highway Code, specifically Rule 57, states that "when in a vehicle, make sure dogs or other animals are suitably restrained so they cannot distract you while you are driving or injure you, or themselves, if you stop quickly." Driving with an unrestrained pet is treated as driving without due care and attention, which can result in a fine of up to £5,000, nine penalty points, or even a driving ban.

Beyond the Highway Code, pet owners should be aware of the Animal Welfare Act 2006, which makes it a criminal offence to cause unnecessary suffering to an animal. Leaving a pet in a hot car, for example, falls under this legislation. According to the RSPCA, a car can reach 47°C on a 22°C day within an hour, making ventilation and shade genuinely life-or-death considerations.

As Loyal & Loved explains, "suitably restrained" is not defined precisely in law, which means the choice of restraint method, whether a harness, a crate, or a boot guard, is largely left to the owner's judgement. However, the British Veterinary Association (BVA) recommends crash-tested restraints as best practice for 2026, as standard harnesses that have not been independently tested offer little real protection in an accident.

For international travel from the UK, you will need an Animal Health Certificate (AHC), issued by an Official Veterinarian within 10 days of departure, plus proof of rabies vaccination and microchipping. The Veterinary Medicines Directorate and the Animal and Plant Health Agency (APHA) govern these requirements. If you are travelling solely within Great Britain or to Northern Ireland, no documentation is currently required beyond standard microchip compliance under the Microchipping of Dogs (England) Regulations 2015.

What are the safest car restraints and carriers for pets?

The safest option for most dogs is a crash-tested harness or a rigid, secured travel crate; for cats, a well-ventilated hard-sided carrier that is seat-belted in place is strongly recommended. Here is how the main options compare.

Crash-tested harnesses

Not all harnesses are equal. The Centre for Pet Safety (CPS), a US-based independent testing organisation whose findings are widely referenced by UK vets, found that many popular harnesses failed catastrophically in crash simulations. Look for harnesses that have been independently crash-tested. The Sleepypod Clickit Sport and the Kurgo Impact Harness are two frequently cited options that have performed well in independent testing. Prices for quality crash-tested harnesses typically range from £40 to £90 in the UK. You can check current prices on Amazon for a range of options.

Travel crates and carriers

A rigid plastic or metal crate secured to the boot floor or rear seats provides excellent protection. For cats and small dogs, a hard-sided carrier secured with a seatbelt is the gold standard. Soft-sided carriers are lighter and more comfortable for short, calm journeys, but offer less structural protection. Expect to pay £25 to £120 for a decent hard-sided carrier and £80 to £250 or more for a robust dog travel crate. Browse pet travel crates on Amazon to compare sizes and ratings.

Boot guards and barriers

A metal boot guard or dog guard divides the rear boot from the passenger cabin, preventing a dog from being thrown forward in a collision. These are a practical choice for large breeds who travel in the boot. Prices range from £30 to £150 depending on the vehicle fit and material. They do not replace a harness or crate but add a meaningful layer of protection.

Seat covers

Hammock-style seat covers keep your upholstery clean and give dogs a more stable, non-slip surface. They are not a safety restraint on their own and should be used alongside a harness or crate. Good-quality options cost between £20 and £60. See seat cover options on Amazon.

What essential gear do you need for pet car journeys?

Beyond the restraint itself, a well-packed car means a far more comfortable trip for everyone involved. Loyal & Loved found that the most commonly forgotten items on pet road trips are a portable water bowl, a spare lead, and waste bags, so start there.

  • Portable water bowl and water: Pets need access to water every two hours on long journeys. Collapsible silicone bowls are lightweight and pack flat. Cost: £5 to £15. Find collapsible bowls on Amazon.
  • Travel-specific bedding: A familiar-smelling blanket or mat in the crate significantly reduces stress. Use something from home rather than something new.
  • Poo bags and a small spade: Non-negotiable for dogs, especially at motorway services.
  • Spare lead and collar: Leads break, clips fail, and unfamiliar environments mean a dog may pull harder. Always carry a backup.
  • Sunshade for rear windows: Helps regulate temperature, particularly for brachycephalic (flat-faced) breeds like French Bulldogs and Persians, who are especially vulnerable to heat. Cost: £8 to £20. Browse car sunshades on Amazon.
  • A GPS tracker: If your dog slips the lead at a service station, a real-time tracker is invaluable. See our guide on choosing a GPS tracker for safety.
  • Pet first aid kit: Covered in more detail below, but do not leave home without one.
  • Calming aid if needed: Discussed in the anxiety section, but worth packing in advance.

How do you prepare your pet for car travel?

Preparation is everything: a dog or cat who has been gradually introduced to the car will travel far more calmly than one who only enters a vehicle for vet trips. The process is called desensitisation and counter-conditioning, and it is well-supported by animal behaviour science.

For dogs

Start by letting your dog explore the stationary car with the engine off, rewarding calm behaviour with high-value treats. Progress to sitting in the car with the engine running, then short two-minute drives around the block, then gradually longer trips. The Dogs Trust recommends this process should take at least two to four weeks for anxious dogs. Always end journeys somewhere fun, like a park, rather than the vet, so the car becomes a positive cue.

For cats

Cats find car travel inherently stressful because they are territorial animals who experience leaving home as a threat. According to International Cat Care, the best approach is to leave the carrier out at home permanently as a sleeping spot, making it a familiar, safe space rather than a once-in-a-blue-moon surprise. Spray the carrier with a synthetic feline pheromone product like Feliway Classic 30 minutes before travel. Cost for a Feliway spray: approximately £18 to £25 from Viovet or veterinary practices.

Motion sickness

Motion sickness in pets is common, particularly in younger animals, as the vestibular system (the inner ear mechanism responsible for balance) is still developing. Signs include excessive drooling, yawning, lethargy, and vomiting. The licensed veterinary medicine Cerenia (maropitant) is available in the UK on prescription and is highly effective for dogs. Speak to your vet before any long journey if you suspect your pet is prone to car sickness. Never administer human travel sickness medication without veterinary advice.

Which UK train operators are pet-friendly and how do you book?

The good news is that most UK train operators allow dogs and small pets, often at no extra charge. The rules do vary between operators, so it is worth checking before you travel.

National Rail general policy

Under National Rail conditions of travel, dogs and cats are generally permitted on all services. Most operators allow up to two dogs per passenger, travelling free of charge. Dogs must be kept under control at all times; on busy services, they may be asked to sit on the floor rather than on seats. Cats must travel in a secure carrier.

Key operator policies (2026)

  • Avanti West Coast: Up to two dogs per passenger, free of charge. Cats and small animals in carriers permitted. Dogs must be kept on a lead.
  • LNER: Dogs travel free. Cats in carriers permitted. Guide and assistance dogs always welcome. Peak services can be very busy, so travelling off-peak is advisable.
  • Great Western Railway (GWR): Dogs free, up to two per person. Cats in carriers free. Recommends booking seats in advance on longer routes like London to Penzance.
  • ScotRail: Dogs travel free with a valid ticket holder. Up to two dogs permitted. Cats in carriers free.
  • Southeastern and Southern: Dogs free, cats in carriers free. On some Southeastern high-speed (HS1) services, check specific terms as they can differ.
  • TransPennine Express: Dogs free of charge, on a lead at all times. Cats in secure carriers permitted.

Practical booking tips

  1. Book a specific seat rather than travelling in an open carriage, as this gives you more control over your pet's space.
  2. Choose off-peak travel where possible to avoid crowded carriages.
  3. Bring a mat or blanket for your dog to lie on, as train floors can be cold and slippery.
  4. Carry water and a portable bowl; buffet cars do not reliably stock pet-appropriate water containers.
  5. Check the operator's website on the day of travel; policies can be updated and some peak-period restrictions apply.

Which UK ferry services accept pets and what are the rules?

Several major UK ferry operators welcome pets, either in kennels, in your vehicle, or in designated pet-friendly cabins. Policies differ significantly between operators, so always confirm directly before booking.

Key operators and routes

  • Brittany Ferries: One of the most pet-friendly operators in Europe. Routes include Portsmouth to Caen, Portsmouth to Cherbourg, and Plymouth to Roscoff. Pet cabins (called "Pets Welcome" cabins) allow up to two pets to stay with their owners. Cost for a pet cabin supplement: approximately £25 to £50 per crossing depending on route and season. Dogs must be vaccinated against rabies and have a valid AHC for entry into France.
  • P&O Ferries: Operates the Dover to Calais and Hull to Rotterdam routes. Pets must remain in the vehicle on the Dover to Calais crossing (approximately 90 minutes), which raises welfare concerns on hot days. On the longer Hull to Rotterdam overnight route, pet kennels are available for approximately £25 to £35 extra per crossing.
  • DFDS: Dover to Calais and Dover to Dunkirk. Similar to P&O; pets remain in vehicles on short crossings. On the Newcastle to Amsterdam overnight ferry, pet kennels are available.
  • Irish Ferries: Holyhead to Dublin and Pembroke to Rosslare. Pets are permitted in vehicles or in on-board kennels. Pet cabin supplements apply on some routes. No AHC is required for travel between Great Britain and Ireland, but pets must be microchipped and vaccinated against rabies. Confirm current APHA guidance before travelling, as rules have been updated post-Brexit.
  • Stena Line: Fishguard to Rosslare, Holyhead to Dublin, and Cairnryan to Belfast. Pets permitted in vehicles. Belfast route: no international health certificate required. Kennels available on some routes for a small supplement.
  • Caledonian MacBrayne (CalMac): Serves Scottish islands including Mull, Skye, Islay, and the Outer Hebrides. Dogs permitted on deck or in vehicles. No additional pet fee. No documentation required for travel within Scotland.

Tips for ferry travel with pets

  • Book a pet cabin or kennel well in advance, as these fill up quickly, especially in summer.
  • For crossings where pets must stay in the vehicle, park in a ventilated position and leave a sunshade up. On longer crossings, ask a crew member to accompany you to check on your pet.
  • Feed your pet a light meal at least two to four hours before departure to reduce the risk of seasickness.
  • Bring familiar bedding to the kennel to reduce stress.
  • Confirm documentation requirements with the operator at least two weeks before departure, especially for routes involving France, the Netherlands, or other EU destinations.

How do you manage pet anxiety during travel?

Travel anxiety in pets is one of the most common concerns owners raise, and it is entirely manageable with the right approach. According to Loyal & Loved, the most effective strategies combine gradual habituation, environmental enrichment, and, where appropriate, veterinary-approved calming support.

Non-medication strategies

  • Familiar scent: Place an unwashed item of your clothing in the carrier or crate. Familiar human scent has been shown to reduce cortisol levels in dogs in studies published in the journal Applied Animal Behaviour Science.
  • Covering the carrier: For cats especially, a light cover over the carrier reduces visual stimulation and can significantly lower stress.
  • White noise or calming music: Research from the Scottish SPCA found that reggae and soft rock reduced stress behaviours in shelter dogs. A playlist at low volume can help on longer journeys.
  • Lick mats and chews: Licking is a self-soothing behaviour for dogs. A lick mat spread with a thin layer of dog-safe peanut butter (xylitol-free) can keep an anxious dog occupied. Find lick mats on Amazon.

Calming supplements and products

Several over-the-counter calming products are available in the UK. These are not medicines and are not regulated by the Veterinary Medicines Directorate, but some have reasonable evidence bases. Options include:

  • Adaptil (dogs) and Feliway (cats): Synthetic pheromone products. The Adaptil travel spray costs approximately £15 to £22 and can be applied to bedding 15 minutes before travel.
  • Zylkene (alpha-casozepine): A supplement derived from milk protein with a reasonable evidence base for mild to moderate anxiety. Available from Viovet and most veterinary practices, typically £20 to £35 for a month's supply.
  • YuCalm Dog (from Lintbells): Contains lemon balm, valerian, and L-theanine. Popular with owners and reasonably well-reviewed, though evidence is largely anecdotal. Cost approximately £20 to £35.

For more serious travel anxiety, our dedicated guide on remedies for travel anxiety covers both prescription and non-prescription options in full detail. Always consult your vet before starting any supplement or medication, particularly for older pets or those on existing medication.

What health checks and documentation do you need before travelling with a pet?

For travel within the UK, documentation requirements are minimal, but a pre-travel health check is always a good idea. For travel abroad, the requirements are considerably more involved.

Domestic UK travel

  • Microchip: Compulsory for all dogs in England, Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland under respective microchipping regulations. Cats became subject to mandatory microchipping in England from June 2024 under the Microchipping of Cats and Dogs (England) Regulations 2023.
  • Up-to-date vaccinations: Not legally required for domestic travel, but strongly recommended. Outbreaks of parvovirus and other preventable diseases do occur at rest stops and dog-friendly venues.
  • Flea and tick treatment: Particularly important if travelling to rural or moorland areas. The Itch Pet subscription service offers veterinary-strength spot-on treatments. You can explore options via Itch Pet.

International travel from the UK (2026)

  • Microchip: Must comply with ISO 11784/11785 standard.
  • Rabies vaccination: Required for all EU and many other destinations. Must be administered after microchipping.
  • Animal Health Certificate (AHC): Issued by an Official Veterinarian (OV), valid for entry into EU countries within 10 days of issue. Cost typically £150 to £300, depending on the practice and number of pets.
  • Tapeworm treatment: Required for dogs entering Finland, Ireland, Northern Ireland, Norway, and Malta. Must be administered by a vet one to five days before arrival.
  • Pet travel insurance: Not a legal requirement, but strongly advisable. Read our guide on pet insurance before travelling to understand what to look for in a policy that covers you abroad.

According to Loyal & Loved, the single most common reason pet owners are turned away at the border is an AHC that was issued too early or a rabies vaccine that was administered after microchipping rather than before. Book your vet appointment at least three to four weeks before departure to allow time to resolve any issues.

What emergency supplies and first aid should you carry on a pet journey?

A pet-specific first aid kit is an essential travel item, not an optional extra. The British Veterinary Association recommends all pet owners have basic first aid knowledge and a stocked kit available, particularly for journeys to remote or rural areas.

What to include in your pet travel first aid kit

  • Sterile gauze pads and bandages (self-adhesive cohesive bandage, often called "vet wrap," is particularly useful)
  • Antiseptic wipes and wound spray (chlorhexidine-based, not alcohol wipes)
  • Tick remover hook (essential for rural and moorland areas)
  • Blunt-ended scissors and tweezers
  • Digital thermometer (normal dog temperature: 38.3°C to 39.2°C; normal cat temperature: 38.1°C to 39.2°C)
  • Foil emergency blanket
  • Your vet's phone number and a 24-hour emergency vet number for your destination area
  • A copy of your pet's vaccination records and microchip number
  • Any regular prescription medication with at least two extra days' supply

Pre-assembled pet first aid kits are available from Amazon UK, with decent options starting from around £15 to £35. Check the contents carefully against the list above and supplement as needed.

If your pet is injured or becomes unwell during travel, do not attempt to self-treat beyond basic first aid stabilisation. Use the PDSA's online vet locator or the Vets Now emergency service finder to locate the nearest out-of-hours practice. Knowing the potential cost of emergency vet care while travelling is useful context for ensuring your insurance covers away-from-home incidents.

What are the top tips for a stress-free pet holiday?

A genuinely enjoyable pet holiday comes down to preparation, pacing, and a willingness to adapt the itinerary around your animal's needs rather than treating them as an afterthought.

  1. Plan rest stops every two hours: The RSPCA recommends stopping at least every two hours on long car journeys to allow dogs to stretch, toilet, and drink. Many UK motorway service stations now have designated dog exercise areas.
  2. Book accommodation first, then plan the route: Confirm your accommodation is genuinely pet-friendly before booking anything else. Some listings describe themselves as "pet-friendly" but charge significant fees or restrict pets to certain rooms. Look for verified pet-friendly options via Snaptrip or Cottages.com, both of which have robust pet-friendly filters and genuine owner reviews.
  3. Pack your pet's food from home: Changing diet abruptly is a common cause of upset stomachs during travel. Bring enough of your pet's usual food for the entire trip. If you are worried about nutrition on longer holidays, AATU Dog & Cat Food offers single-protein recipes that are easy on sensitive stomachs. Bella & Duke also offers raw food delivered to your holiday address in the UK if ordered in advance.
  4. Keep routines as normal as possible: Feed, walk, and settle your pet at the same times you would at home. Routine is deeply reassuring for animals in unfamiliar environments.
  5. Bring the familiar: Your dog's bed, your cat's favourite toy, the blanket that smells of home. These objects carry enormous comfort value and take up minimal space.
  6. Check local bylaws on dogs: Many beaches in the UK operate seasonal dog bans (typically May to September on specific sections). Check local council websites before you arrive to avoid disappointment.
  7. Fit a GPS tracker before departure: Unfamiliar environments mean higher escape risk. A real-time GPS tracker for safety gives you genuine peace of mind and has reunited many a lost dog on holiday.
  8. Do not forget parasite prevention: Rural and coastal areas often have higher tick populations. Ensure your pet's flea and tick treatment is up to date before you travel, and check for ticks after every walk. Itch Pet makes it easy to get the right treatment delivered before you go.
  9. Know where the nearest vet is: Look this up before you need it. The RCVS (Royal College of Veterinary Surgeons) has a practice finder on their website and Vets Now operates 24-hour emergency clinics in many UK cities.
  10. Insure before you travel: A policy that does not cover treatment away from your registered vet practice is a policy worth reconsidering. Read our guide on pet insurance before travelling to make sure you are properly covered.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it illegal to travel with an unrestrained dog in the car in the UK?

While there is no specific standalone law against it, the Highway Code Rule 57 requires animals to be "suitably restrained" in vehicles. Failing to comply can lead to prosecution for driving without due care and attention, with fines of up to £5,000 and up to nine penalty points on your licence. It is also a significant safety risk for both the dog and the vehicle's occupants.

Can I take my cat on a UK train?

Yes. Most UK train operators, including LNER, GWR, Avanti West Coast, and ScotRail, permit cats provided they travel in a secure, enclosed carrier. Travel is generally free of charge for cats. Check the specific operator's terms on their website before you travel, as policies can vary slightly between franchises.

Which ferry company is best for travelling with dogs in the UK?

Brittany Ferries is widely regarded as the most pet-friendly operator for routes between the UK and France, offering dedicated "Pets Welcome" cabins where dogs and cats can stay with their owners. For routes to Ireland, both Irish Ferries and Stena Line are reasonable options. For Scottish island travel, Caledonian MacBrayne (CalMac) allows dogs on deck or in vehicles at no extra charge.

Do I need an Animal Health Certificate to travel with my dog from England to Scotland?

No. Travel within Great Britain, including England, Scotland, and Wales, does not require any pet travel documentation. Your dog must be microchipped under the Microchipping of Dogs (England) Regulations 2015, or equivalent Scottish and Welsh legislation, but no health certificate or vaccination records are required for domestic UK travel. An Animal Health Certificate is only needed for travel to EU countries or other international destinations.

What should I do if my dog gets car sick?

Start with practical measures: travel on an empty stomach (fast for two to four hours before departure), ensure good ventilation, and keep the car cool. Position your dog so they can see out of the window, as visual motion cues can reduce nausea. If sickness persists, speak to your vet about the licensed prescription medication Cerenia (maropitant), which is specifically approved for preventing vomiting from motion sickness in dogs. Never give human travel sickness tablets without explicit veterinary guidance, as some ingredients are toxic to dogs.

How do I find pet-friendly holiday accommodation in the UK?

Specialist booking platforms with dedicated pet-friendly filters are your best starting point. Snaptrip and Cottages.com both allow you to filter by pet-friendly status and read verified guest reviews from other pet owners. Always confirm the specific pet policy, including any fees or restrictions, directly with the owner or manager before finalising your booking.
